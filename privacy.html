subbr privacy policy

Last updated: 21 November 2026

subbr is an Android app made by Mypt (“we”). This policy explains what happens to your information when you use it. The short version: subbr collects nothing. It has no internet permission, so it can’t send anything anywhere, and we never see your messages, friends or keys.

What we collect

Nothing. subbr has no account or sign-up, and no ads, analytics, crash reporting or tracking. It has no internet permission (you can check this on its Google Play page), so it can’t contact us or anyone else.

What stays on your phone

To work, subbr keeps the following on your phone, encrypted:

  • the name you choose for yourself;
  • your friends: the names, notes and optional contact details (such as a WhatsApp number, Telegram username or email address) that you type in, and the secret key subbr makes for each friend;
  • your history: the last 10 messages with each friend, with small previews of photos and videos. Messages set to self-destruct leave no text behind;
  • your settings, and whether you have bought Unlimited secrets.

None of this leaves your phone. subbr is left out of Android backups, so it isn’t copied to Google Drive or to a new phone either. This also means we can’t recover it for you.

Permissions

  • Camera: to scan a friend’s QR code and, if you choose, to take a photo to hide a message in. Nothing else.
  • Photos and videos: read only when you pick one with Android’s own picker. subbr can’t see the rest of your gallery.
  • Clipboard: read only when you tap a paste button. When you copy something from subbr, it is marked as sensitive and cleared after about a minute if it is still there.
  • Fingerprint, face or screen lock: optional, to lock subbr. Android checks it; subbr never sees your fingerprint or face.
  • Vibration: small taps when you press buttons.
  • Screenshots: subbr blocks screenshots and screen recordings of itself and stays blank in the recent-apps view.
  • Google Play billing: to buy Unlimited secrets.

Messages you send

You send hidden messages with apps you already use, such as WhatsApp, Telegram or email. Those apps carry them under their own privacy policies, and what they see is an ordinary sentence, photo or video, not your secret. The secret is encrypted with a key that only your phone and your friend’s phone have (XChaCha20-Poly1305, with keys agreed by X25519 and, once you both tap the shield, post-quantum ML-KEM-768; friends who scan each other’s QR code get a random key that never travels). Only someone with that key can read it.

An honest note: a careful inspector can tell that something is probably hidden, but without the key they can’t read it.

Purchases

Unlimited secrets is bought through Google Play. Google handles the payment under the Google Privacy Policy; we never see your card or payment details. subbr only asks Google Play, on your phone, whether you own the unlock. Google gives us the order details it gives every developer (such as the order number, date, product, price and country) so that we can handle refunds; we use them for nothing else.

Statistics from Google Play

Google Play Console shows us totals such as installs, uninstalls and ratings, and crash counts from phones whose owners allow Android to share diagnostics with Google. These come from Google, not from subbr, and don’t tell us who you are.

Children

subbr is meant for adults (18 and over) and isn’t directed at children.

Deleting your information

Everything is on your phone, so you are in control: Settings → Delete everything erases it, and so does uninstalling subbr. We hold no copy, so there is nothing for us to delete.

Deleting a message or clearing a chat in subbr removes it from your phone only. It stays on your friend’s phone and in the chat app it was sent with.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the date at the top. Because subbr collects nothing, we have no way to message you about it.

Contact

Questions about privacy: open an issue on GitHub. Issues are public, so please don’t include anything private.
