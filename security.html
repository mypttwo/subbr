How subbr protects you

The plain version first, then the honest limits, then the details for anyone who wants to check our work.

In plain words

  • Your message is encrypted with the password you share with your friend, then hidden inside a sentence, photo or video.
  • Without the password nobody can read it: not the messaging app, not us, not someone who gets hold of the message.
  • On your phone, everything subbr keeps is encrypted with a key held in the phone's secure hardware. Optionally, subbr only opens with your fingerprint, face or screen lock.
  • subbr has no internet permission, so it can't leak anything by itself.

The limits

  • Hidden, not invisible to experts. A careful inspector can tell that a text, photo or video probably carries something hidden. They can't read it.
  • The password is everything. A short or guessable password can be found by trying many. Use the suggested ones, and share them in person or on a call, not in the chat you use for messages.
  • Timers are enforced by subbr on your friend's phone. The hidden message stays in the chat; someone who reinstalls subbr or changes the phone's clock could open it again.
  • A phone that is unlocked and in someone else's hands can open subbr unless the lock in Settings is on.

The details

  • Key from password: Argon2id (libsodium, 64 MiB, 3 passes) with a fresh random 16-byte salt for every message. Passwords are Unicode-normalised (NFC).
  • Encryption: XChaCha20-Poly1305 with a random 24-byte nonce; the header (version, parameters, salt, nonce) is authenticated. The message is compressed, then padded to a size bucket so its length leaks little.
  • Timers travel inside the encrypted message, so they can't be changed without the password.
  • Text: the encrypted bytes become invisible Unicode format characters (word joiner, invisible operators) spread between the words, with Reed-Solomon error correction and a checksum.
  • Photos: either a JPEG that survives messaging apps' recompression (the message is spread redundantly over positions chosen from a key derived from the password), or a lossless PNG sent as a file for longer messages. Before sharing, subbr checks that a harshly recompressed copy still reveals.
  • Videos: the message is embedded in every frame, the result is checked after a harsh re-encode, and only then offered for sending.
  • On the phone: friends, passwords and history are one document encrypted with XChaCha20-Poly1305 under a random 256-bit key stored in the Android Keystore; with the lock on, that key is released only after a fingerprint, face or screen-lock check.
  • Wrong password or tampering always gives the same “can't open” result, never partial text.
